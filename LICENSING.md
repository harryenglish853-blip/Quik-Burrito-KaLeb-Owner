# Licensing and content provenance

An inventory of everything in this site that someone other than the studio or
the restaurant may have rights in, so both parties can see exactly what is being
relied on.

**This is an engineering inventory, not legal advice.** Nobody here is a lawyer.
It records what was used and under what terms so a lawyer can review it quickly
if you want that.

### GSAP — the one worth understanding

GSAP 3.15 ships under GreenSock's own **Standard "No Charge" License**, not MIT.
Since Webflow's 2025 change it is free for commercial use, explicitly including
**billed client websites**. The prohibited use is building a no-code visual
animation tool that competes with Webflow's animation builder.

A restaurant website is squarely inside what the licence permits. Current terms:
https://gsap.com/standard-license

If that dependency is ever unwanted, GSAP is used in exactly one place —
`scenes/CineScene.tsx` — and the site already degrades to a still composition
per chapter without it.

## 5. Written content

All copy on the site was written for this project. Menu item names and
descriptions are the restaurant's own, taken from their public menu listings;
facts are short and functional rather than creative expression.

Campaign lines ("Big flavor. Made quik.", "Dip into something better.") were
written here and are marked as campaign copy in `data/brand.ts`, not presented
as pre-existing slogans.

No third-party text is reproduced anywhere.

## 6. Customer reviews

**No review is quoted on the site.** `data/reviews.ts` ships empty and the page
links to Google and Yelp instead.

That is deliberate and worth keeping in mind before changing it: republishing
reviews pulled from Google or Yelp is governed by those platforms' terms, and
attributing a quote to a named person carries its own exposure. If reviews are
added later, take them with attribution and a link back to the source, which is
what the data structure is already shaped for.

## 7. What the site collects — verified, not assumed

Measured on a real page load:

- **Third-party hosts contacted: none.** No analytics, no fonts, no trackers, no
  social scripts. The Instagram embed loads only if a visitor taps play.
- **Cookies set: none.**
- **Browser storage on load: none.** A single `localStorage` key is written only
  when a visitor actively chooses a location, and it stays on their device.
- **Forms and inputs: none.** The site cannot collect personal data because
  there is nowhere to enter any.
- **Analytics:** `lib/analytics.ts` fires only if the owner later adds GA4 or
  Meta Pixel, and is consent-gated. Nothing is loaded today.

This is about as low as privacy exposure gets for a public website. If analytics
or a contact form is added later, that changes and a privacy policy becomes
necessary.

## 8. Accessibility

US restaurant websites do attract ADA/WCAG complaints, so this was built and
checked for it. Verified across all six routes: exactly one `<h1>` per page, no
skipped heading levels, alt text on every image, an accessible name on every
link and button, no tap target under 40px, `rel="noopener"` on every new-tab
link, a visible keyboard focus ring, a skip link, and a reduced-motion mode that
disables the scroll choreography while leaving ordering fully usable.

That is a solid baseline, not a certification. A formal audit (axe, or a manual
screen-reader pass) is the next step if you want one.
